# Recorder setup

The recorder's gain matters more than anything the software does. If the gain is too high, loud
music distorts the recording ("clipping"), and the speech it buries can't be recovered afterwards.

## Tonfarb A20 settings

Menu names vary slightly between units.

| Setting | Set to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gain (Sensitivity) | 1–2 in a club, 1 near a stage or big speakers | Any higher and the bass overloads the recording |
| Format (Quality) | WAV 1536 kbps | Keeps the detail that makes muffled speech readable; the 512 kbps default loses it |
| Noise reduction | Off | It's made for steady hum and smears speech when there's music |
| Recording scene | Custom/Manual, else Interview/Dictation | Meeting, Lecture and Conference raise the gain. Check the gain again after choosing |
| Audio recording aid | Off, if it's auto gain or voice enhance | Auto gain undoes your low gain setting |
| AVR (voice activation) | Off | Music keeps triggering it, and it cuts off the start of sentences |
| Segmented recording | 60 min | About 690 MB per file |
| Timed recording | Off | Start recording by hand |
| Date and time | Correct to the minute | Files are named by their start time, which gives the transcript its clock times |

## Wearing it

Use the clip-on mic that came with it. The closer it is to your mouth, the louder your voice is
compared with the music: each time you halve the distance, your voice gains about 6 dB.

- **Button-up shirt:** clip it just inside the placket at the second button, facing up.
- **T-shirt:** tape it inside the neckline at the centre.
- **Cable:** run it inside your shirt to the recorder in a pocket, with a small taped loop just
  below the mic so tugs don't reach it.
- **Rustle:** keep jackets from rubbing over the mic. Cotton is quieter than synthetic fabric, and a
  stick-on fur cover (such as Rycote Undercovers) helps most.
- **No clip-on mic:** recorder in a chest pocket, mic end up. Never in a trouser pocket.

## Before you go out

- [ ] Battery charged, and about 690 MB free for each hour you'll record
- [ ] Record 30 seconds near a speaker while talking, then listen on earphones. Crunchy or distorted
      means lower the gain one step and try again; still distorted at the lowest gain means stand
      further from the speakers

## On the night and after

- Start a new file (slide record off and on) when a conversation starts, so it's easy to find
  later. Keep each file at least 5 seconds long, or the A20 shows "Format Error".
- Talk away from the speakers when you can: a few metres makes a real difference.
- Copy the WAV files to your computer the next day, and keep the originals unedited.

## Other recorders and phones

The same rules apply: the lowest gain that doesn't distort; WAV, or the highest quality available;
noise reduction, auto gain and voice activation off; the clock set; the mic close to your mouth.

Recorders that record 32-bit float can't clip in the recording itself, so the gain setting matters
much less (a mic can still distort at extreme levels, so do the listening test anyway). Pocket
recorders with a clip-on mic that offer it include the Deity PR-2, Tascam DR-10L Pro and Zoom F2.
Check that the mode you use records 32-bit float: some offer it only in mono.

## The difference it makes

Three nights on the same A20:

| Night | Settings | Audio clipped | Conversation recovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Club | default gain, 512 kbps, recorder in a pocket | 8.3% | 15 min from 3.5 hours |
| Club | gain 1–2, WAV 1536 kbps, clip-on mic | 2.3% | 37 min from 1.6 hours |
| Outdoor festival | gain 1–2, WAV 1536 kbps, clip-on mic | 4.8% | 72 min from 3.7 hours |

At the festival, the hour nearest a stage still clipped 11.8% at gain 1–2; hence gain 1 near stages.
